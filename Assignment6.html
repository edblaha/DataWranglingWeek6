  1. How would you interpret (in other words, explain to a general audience) what Mondak and Sanders found in figures 1, 2, and 5?

Figure 1 demonstrates that from 1976-1998, the American public became more tolerant of various groups. Figure 2 demonstrated that this increase in tolerance was greater for homosexuality than for racism (which is itself an intolerant belief system).

Figure 5 demonstrates that a substantial minority of respondents hold 0 of 15 intolerant beliefs. This pattern may suggest that these individuals hold a broader, consistent orientation toward tolerance rather than attitudes driven primarily by biases toward particular groups. One possible explanation is that they place greater value on principles such as freedom of speech. The relatively even distribution across the remaining 14 categories, by contrast, suggests that responses may be shaped more by individuals’ attitudes toward specific groups than by an ideology of tolerance. This minority has increased over time, suggesting that an ideology of total tolerance is growing.

  1. Has anything changed since 1998 that would alter the substantive takeaway points in their findings?

Tolerance has continued to increase, but intolerance of racism, which itself a hateful and intolerant ideology, has increased. Tolerance seems to be modulated by the harm caused to others by the group in question. The proportion of people with at least one intolerant belief has thus increased (extension of 5), but this is likely because fewer people are tolerant of racists.
